Meta lanza Muse Wonder y Muse Wonder Full, sus primeros modelos de frontera

Meta presentó este lunes sus dos modelos más capaces hasta la fecha, construidos por Meta Superintelligence Labs para trabajo agente de largo alcance. Wonder Full alcanza 56 puntos en el índice de Artificial Analysis, cerca de Claude Opus 5.5, pero la versión más fuerte solo estará disponible para suscriptores de Muse Code.

Meta anunció el 12 de octubre la familia Muse Wonder, sus primeros modelos de clase frontera, provenientes de Meta Superintelligence Labs, el laboratorio que dirige Alexandr Wang. Lo más interesante es la estrategia comercial. Pero Muse Wonder Full, la versión que vence a sus rivales en benchmarks de programación como SWE-Marathon, no estará en la API: solo los suscriptores de los planes de Muse Code de 50 y 200 dólares mensuales podrán usarlo. Ambos incorporan «Active Memory» (retener hechos clave entre conversaciones) y «Active Focus» (persistir en problemas difíciles, probar alternativas y consultar antes de actuar), con contexto de alrededor de un millón de tokens.

El equipo de LeCun propone H-JEPA, un modelo del mundo jerárquico para la planificación visual de robots

Un paper en arXiv con Yann LeCun como coautor presenta un esquema de entrenamiento para un modelo del mundo que planifica por capas: cada nivel predice más lejos en el futuro y sus predicciones se convierten en submetas. En Visual AntMaze, la tasa de éxito subió del 18 % al 73 %.

Wancong Zhang, Basile Terver, Michael Rabbat, Yann LeCun y Randall Balestriero publicaron en arXiv H-JEPA, un esquema de entrenamiento de extremo a extremo para un modelo del mundo JEPA jerárquico condicionado a acciones, donde cada capa predice más lejos en el futuro en su espacio latente aprendido. La planificación corre de arriba hacia abajo: las predicciones de cada capa se convierten en submetas para la capa inferior.

En cuatro entornos simulados de navegación y manipulación, la planificación jerárquica superó al JEPA plano; en Visual AntMaze, una estructura de tres capas elevó la tasa de éxito del 18 % al 73 % usando menos cómputo para planificar. Bajo supervisión de dinámica inversa, el método escala a videos reales de robots del conjunto DROID. Es otro paso en la apuesta de LeCun por los modelos del mundo como vía a una IA menos dependiente del lenguaje.

LeCun's team proposes H-JEPA, a hierarchical world model for visual robot planning

An arXiv paper co-authored by Yann LeCun presents a training scheme for a world model that plans in layers: each level predicts further into the future, and its predictions become sub-goals. In Visual AntMaze, success rose from 18% to 73%.

Wancong Zhang, Basile Terver, Michael Rabbat, Yann LeCun and Randall Balestriero published H-JEPA on arXiv, an end-to-end training scheme for a hierarchical action-conditioned JEPA world model where each layer predicts further into the future in its learned latent space. Planning runs top-down: each layer's predictions become sub-goals for the layer below.

Across four simulated navigation/manipulation environments, hierarchical planning outperformed flat JEPA; in Visual AntMaze, a three-layer structure raised success from 18% to 73% while needing less compute for planning. Under inverse-dynamics supervision it scales to real robot videos in the DROID dataset. It is another step in LeCun's bet on world models as a path to AI less dependent on language.

The International Monetary Fund warned in its Global Financial Stability Report, presented at the Bangkok meetings, that "an AI market correction will affect the real economy." The report sees "growing evidence" that AI capabilities may not require as much investment — or deliver as large economic gains — as expected.

The most cited stat: hyperscalers now represent 10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from under 1% in 2024. If AI investment momentum slows, that leverage could force selling and volatility. At the same meetings, IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva said AI could affect about 40% of jobs globally over time (up to 60% in advanced economies). It is the biggest institutional voice validating the AI-bubble thesis, a week after Beijing warned about excessive investment.

US liability wave: who pays when AI agents go rogue — bills in Congress and New York

The debate over AI developer liability intensified in Washington: Senators Hawley and Murphy introduced a bill holding agent operators liable for hacking incidents, Rep. Trahan filed her civil-liability act on October 11, and New York already passed a law holding developers liable for cyberattacks enabled by their systems.

The question of who answers when an AI agent goes wrong escalated in US politics. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argued existing liability laws suffice — "the best way to guarantee safety is that the creators are liable for what they build" — but legal experts cited by Bloomberg warn the main US hacking law requires proof of intent, hard to pin on an autonomous agent.

In Congress, Senators Josh Hawley and Chris Murphy introduced a bill to hold AI agent operators and developers liable for hacking incidents, and Rep. Lori Trahan filed her act creating a federal civil cause of action on October 11. New York moved first: Gov. Kathy Hochul signed A.B. 6453 on October 9, holding advanced-AI developers liable for cyberattacks enabled by their systems, requiring safety plans and 72-hour incident alerts, effective January 2027. It is a different angle from the White House incident-reporting mandate covered last week: this is about legal liability and financial exposure.

Von der Leyen proposes 'Only Once': using AI to cut repetitive EU business reporting

The European Commission president proposed in a closed-door speech that EU companies report data to officials just once, with AI helping administrations use it efficiently. It is the first time she has openly proposed using AI to lighten business paperwork.

Ursula von der Leyen proposed "Only Once": EU companies would report data to officials just once, with AI helping administrations use it efficiently, avoiding repetitive reporting. The proposal came in a speech at a closed-door European People's Party gathering in Taormina, Sicily, per Politico.

There are no details on how the AI system would work, which companies it would cover, or timing, and in the same speech she urged governments to sign a pact against "gold-plating" (adding national rules on top of EU law). It is the first time von der Leyen has openly proposed AI as a tool to cut administrative burden, in line with the March EU Inc. proposal for a single company structure across member states — though that plan did not mention AI.
