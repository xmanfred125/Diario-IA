Anthropic measures its agents' effect on its own workforce: junior share of hires falls from 27% to 9%

An Anthropic Institute report based on internal data shows the junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9%, that interactive agent hours now run six times employees' own hours, and that code commits per engineer are up fivefold.

A report published October 16 by the Anthropic Institute, How employment is changing at Anthropic, turns the company's own workplace into a case study of the AI transition. Drawing on internal headcount, hiring, calendar and AI-usage data plus 17 staff interviews, its authors -- Nathan Wilmers, Regina Catipon, Maxim Massenkoff and Peter McCrory -- document a sharp shift: junior employees fell from 21% to 14% of technical departments since early 2025, and the junior share of new technical hires dropped from 27% to 9%, with junior postings plunging since December 2025. In an April survey, about a third of machine-learning managers and a fifth of software-engineering managers said they were hiring less because of AI. By August, interactive agent hours directed by employees ran six times the employees' own hours; agent work hours had first passed human hours in March. Code commits per engineer ran at five times their pace of six months earlier -- and the authors checked quality, finding no difference in how often agent-written versus human-written lines were later removed or reverted, while conceding that more code does not necessarily mean better code. The gains spread beyond engineering: lawyers processed more contracts, accounts payable cleared more invoices, salespeople closed more deals, and meta-work -- building and monitoring agent pipelines -- grew from 10% to 22% of interactive Claude sessions. Caveats matter: this is a single-company self-portrait by the vendor whose agents it credits, built on its own data, over a year in which Anthropic also roughly tripled in size. The authors state plainly that the report describes the trend and does not claim AI caused it.
